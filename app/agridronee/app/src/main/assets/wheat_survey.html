🌾 Wheat Field Drone Report

Executive Summary

This report details a drone-assisted crop analysis conducted on March 15, 2024 over the North Field (25 acres). The survey focused on identifying crop health distribution, detecting potential pest threats, and analyzing soil moisture conditions. Overall, the field shows good crop health, with balanced moisture levels and a low risk of pest activity.

Crop Health Overview

Health Analysis

The wheat crop in the North Field is thriving, with 70% rated as healthy. This is attributed to well-managed fertilization and consistent rainfall during the early growth period. 20% of the crop exhibits minor stress symptoms, likely due to slight nutrient variations and earlier cold exposure. Only 10% shows signs of poor growth, mainly located near the western edge where drainage and water access are limited.

Disease & Pest Detection

Pest & Disease Risk

The drone scan did not detect any major disease clusters. Fungal activity was negligible, affecting under 10% of the field in shaded areas. Pest risk is currently low, though a few aphid hotspots were observed in denser crop zones. No action is required at this time, but biweekly monitoring is recommended to prevent escalation.

Soil Moisture Levels

Soil Moisture Report

The average soil moisture across the field was measured at 65%, indicating a well-balanced hydration state. The eastern section retains moisture efficiently due to natural soil compaction, while a few dry patches in the central strip were identified—possibly due to uneven irrigation pressure. There are no signs of overwatering or waterlogging.

Wheat Growth Stages

Crop Development

Most of the crop is in the tillering stage (55%), showing uniformity and vigor. About 30% has entered the stem elongation stage, and a small percentage (15%) is lagging slightly. These areas should be monitored for potential seedling issues or localized nutrient gaps. The overall maturity forecast is on track for a late-May harvest.

Recommendations

Maintain current irrigation cycle with minor adjustments in central strip.
Begin foliar application in lagging zones to promote uniform growth.
Conduct pest scouting in dense canopy areas.
Schedule a follow-up drone scan by the end of March.
No fungicide needed unless fungal activity increases.

Conclusion

The North Field shows strong crop potential, with minimal pest or disease pressure and optimal moisture levels. Regular monitoring and light agronomic adjustments will help sustain the current trajectory and ensure a healthy wheat yield this season.
