🌽 Corn Field Drone Report

Executive Summary

This drone survey was conducted on March 10, 2024 over the Central Field (40 acres), planted with corn. The primary objective was to assess vegetation health, pest presence, and soil moisture distribution. The field currently exhibits fair overall health, with moderate pest activity and signs of early water stress.

Crop Health Overview

Health Analysis

The Central Field showed 55% healthy corn, with vibrant leaf color and strong NDVI signatures. 30% of the field showed moderate stress, including uneven canopy height and early signs of nutrient deficiency. The remaining 15% was categorized as poor health, primarily in the southern region, likely due to poor drainage and compacted soil layers.

Pest & Disease Detection

Pest Activity & Disease

The drone detected moderate pest activity—especially along the northern tree line where aphids and early-stage armyworms were identified. Fungal hotspots were visible in 10% of the field, mostly in moisture-retentive areas. Preventive spraying is advised within the next 5–7 days to avoid escalation.

Soil Moisture Levels

Moisture & Irrigation

Average soil moisture across the Central Field was measured at 55%, which is slightly below ideal for early corn development. The southwestern zone is significantly drier, likely due to blocked irrigation nozzles. No signs of over-irrigation were detected. Prompt irrigation balancing is necessary to avoid crop stress during vegetative stages.

Corn Growth Stages

Growth & Development

The field has a mixed growth stage distribution. 50% of the corn is in the V3–V5 stage, showing consistent leaf development. 35% is in earlier stages (V1–V2), often in drier or stressed regions. The remaining 15% is lagging behind, mostly in shaded or compacted soil areas. Uniformity can be improved with localized inputs.

Recommendations

Apply insecticide in high-risk aphid zones (north boundary).
Flush and calibrate irrigation in the southwest sector.
Add foliar nitrogen spray in lagging patches.
Reassess pest pressure in 7 days with follow-up drone scan.
Improve aeration in compacted zones using shallow tillage.

Conclusion

The Central Field corn crop is progressing under fair conditions. Moisture stress and pest activity are manageable with immediate actions. Continued surveillance and mid-season nutrient boosts will help stabilize growth and improve overall field uniformity heading into the reproductive phase.
